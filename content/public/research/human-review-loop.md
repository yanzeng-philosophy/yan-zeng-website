The right first step is not to automate the publication of every useful thought. It is to build a small review loop: AI generates candidate drafts, a person reviews them, and only selected pieces become public.

This matters because AI-assisted writing can blur the boundary between exploration and publication. A conversation may contain a promising argument, but it may also contain private context, temporary judgment, weak formulation, or claims that still need evidence. Treating all generated text as publishable would confuse intellectual productivity with public responsibility.

A better workflow separates three stages. First, raw material belongs in a private space. Second, AI-generated drafts belong in a draft space where they can be revised and rejected. Third, only reviewed material belongs in the public site.

The point is not to slow down thinking. The point is to make publication intentional. A public research notebook should accumulate ideas, but it should also preserve the difference between having an idea, testing an idea, and standing behind an idea in public.
