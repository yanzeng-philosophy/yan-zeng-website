The central question is not only whether people trust AI systems too much or too little. A deeper issue is how AI-mediated practices redistribute the burdens of judgment, monitoring, explanation, and repair.

When a system gives recommendations, ranks options, or drafts text, the human user is often described as the final authority. But this description can hide an unfair transfer of responsibility. The user may be expected to catch errors without being given the time, expertise, institutional power, or situational clarity needed to do so.

This research log treats trust as a social and institutional relation rather than a simple attitude toward a tool. The useful question becomes: under what conditions is it fair to ask a person to carry the burden of oversight?
