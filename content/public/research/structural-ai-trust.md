summary: "A research note on why AI trust should be analyzed through role structure, not only system properties."

Many approaches to AI trust begin with system properties. They ask whether an AI system is transparent, reliable, robust, fair, or explainable. These properties matter, but they do not settle the full normative question.

The missing question is structural: what role is the human participant being asked to occupy within the dependence relation? A user may be told to verify, supervise, correct, or take final responsibility for an AI-mediated outcome. But that role may be unfair if the person lacks the time, expertise, authority, or institutional support needed to perform it well.

This shifts the analysis of trust. The question is not simply whether the system has enough good properties to deserve reliance. It is also whether the human-AI arrangement distributes burdens in a way that makes trust practically inhabitable.

On this view, unfair role-burden allocation can undermine rational trust even when a system looks impressive at the level of transparency or performance. Trust in AI-mediated practices is therefore not just a relation between a user and a technical artifact. It is a structured dependence relation shaped by institutions, interfaces, expectations, and responsibility assignments.
